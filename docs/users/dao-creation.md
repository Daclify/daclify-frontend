# Saved DAO orders and claim links

After successful creation or claiming, the portal opens the DAO workspace. Locked keys do not redirect you to account settings. Use **Set up approvals** in the workspace when you are ready to enable signing, then return to the same DAO. Signing and private-document access still require the existing authorization and keys.

**Save this order for my own account → Copy my order link** saves the address of your order so your own account can resume it. The address is also shown in a read-only field for manual copying. It is not an invitation and does not let another account claim the DAO.

If you reviewed a free shared setup and then decide it is for someone else, leave it uncreated. **Prepare this setup for someone else** is expanded for an eligible Daclify DAO executive. Use its invitation approval flow and **Create claim link**, then share **Copy claim link** with the recipient. The link copies the immutable saved settings into a separate seven-day invitation. The first person to confirm it with their Daclify account creates the DAO and becomes its sole initial member, administrator and executive. The issuer does not join.

If a saved-order link is rejected, the portal explains that it is personal and hides the blank new-DAO form. **Use the original account** preserves the order address while you sign out and sign in with the account that saved it; **Retry order** checks that order again. A recipient should ask the sender for a claim invitation. **Start a new setup** explicitly leaves the rejected link and opens an independent setup; it neither claims nor changes the original order.

The original order remains unchanged and unused. Do not create both paths unless you intend two separate DAOs. Paid receipts and already-reserved claim orders do not offer this shortcut; it never transfers a payment, changes founding keys or rewrites an existing order. Account access/unlocking returns to the same saved order.

After a DAO has been created, a new claim link cannot transfer that existing DAO. Use its normal membership and role process to admit and appoint the recipient, then complete a separate handover. Its original creation and governance history remains recorded.
