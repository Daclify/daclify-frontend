# Exploring contract accounts

Open **Status → Contracts**, or use **Explore contract map** in Overview. The checked timestamp applies to the platform snapshot, including release hashes and public permissions.

The diagram includes contract and service account names. Relay is the creation/settlement service and Fees is the fee treasury. When a service account has no deployed code, the panel is **Service Account Details** and release status is **No contract code**. Other zero-code accounts without a required release pin also show that state; a missing pinned contract remains a mismatch. Click a name, or focus it and press Enter, to switch **Contract Account Details** below it. That panel contains the selected account's RAM, CPU and NET, its permission tree and its release details.

Solid amber arrows point from an account delegating authority to the contract account whose permission it uses. Dashed green arrows point from an account permission to another contract whose actions are linked to that permission. These are reported permission relationships; they do not establish that a contract has called another contract. Hover or focus a contract for the specific permission/action descriptions. Dotted cream arrows show the configured Hub registry, creation settler, fee treasury and name service. These come from the network and platform settings, even when the accounts have no cross-contract permission links. A configured role does not add an account to another account's owner/active authority; contract code can still trust configured actors for particular operations. Accounts without any reported connection remain visible without invented lines.

The permission tree nests each child beneath its actual parent. Its connector ends at the final child row, with custom permissions continuing beneath their own parent. Each row shows the permission name, a threshold badge and its contributors with +weight labels. Full public keys stay visible inside their permission rows; the same key can appear in both owner and active. Selecting a permission highlights its contributors. Selecting a public key highlights every permission listing it and the corresponding names in the contract diagram. The connection details below the tree let you inspect a matching permission on another account. Selecting a delegated permission lets you follow its definition when that contract and permission were returned.

`owner` is the root; `active` normally has `owner` as its parent. Each permission has its own authority definition. A parent may satisfy a child's minimum permission; an active signer does not gain owner authority through that hierarchy. A shared key can separately contribute to both authorities, but their thresholds still apply. `eosio.code` represents authorization contributed by deployed contract execution. Delay entries contribute weight after the stated transaction delay.

External delegated account authorities may not be expanded, and contracts may impose additional checks. Linked-action chips such as `eosio::claimrewards` appear when the public RPC reports them and its parent and authority match the status permission. `contract::*` represents a reported wildcard link. Up to three action chips are visible; use **more actions** to expand longer lists with touch or keyboard. These links come from the separately checked account reading; absent chips or lines do not establish that no action links exist. A release hash match identifies an artifact; it does not certify its safety. Use the bundled **Permission guide** for worked governance and module examples.

The tree is the single authority view. **Clear selection** resets signer and permission highlights. Contract names, permissions, contributors and observed connections are keyboard buttons. Rows adapt to narrow screens and enlarged text. Open **Release details** for full code hashes and release pins.

RAM and NET are bytes; CPU is microseconds. These are contract-account totals, shared by hosted DAOs, rather than per-DAO charges or transaction estimates. The CPU/NET meters show reported used capacity; resource limits can change. Unlimited, unknown, zero capacity and over-capacity are labelled explicitly.

Resource readings are separate public RPC reads. The app first checks the RPC chain ID and rejects account-name mismatches. A failed resource read leaves permission data available, labels CPU/NET unavailable and retains RAM from the marked platform snapshot. **Refresh resources** retries those reads; **Refresh status** refreshes platform permissions and resources. Refresh preserves the selected contract and release disclosure where available. Changing deployment clears old data, and late responses are discarded.

Use **Resources** beside **Permission guide** to open the resource card. Its RAM, CPU and NET views list every configured account with used/free percentages and amounts. Contract totals exclude known accounts with no code; those service accounts remain listed and can receive resources. Missing account readings make complete totals unavailable. The card measures whole contract accounts, including deployed WASM, ABI, permissions and tables, rather than a single DAO's allocations.

The RAM dollar figure estimates the current replacement cost of occupied bytes, including the system RAM fee. It is not historical spending or immediately sellable RAM. CPU and NET figures value their TLOS stake. Dollars use a positive published core or configured Names rate observed within the last 15 minutes; expired or missing rates show **USD unavailable**. Testnet/local dollars are mainnet-price references; their tokens have no cash value.

Select the recipient account. The RAM slider starts from its current capacity: **100%** leaves it unchanged, and **200%** doubles the target capacity. A contract at 99% usage would be near 49.5% after doubling, subject to the RAM received at execution. CPU/NET sliders instead scale the existing TLOS stake; capacity changes with network conditions. A known zero stake uses an explicit additional-TLOS input.

Choose **Connect Anchor** or **Change payer** to select the paying native account. **Prepare purchase** reads the current system contract, RAM market and payer balance, then shows the recipient and fixed TLOS amount. **Buy RAM with Anchor** or **Stake CPU/NET with Anchor** asks that account to approve the exact transaction. The payer needs its `active` permission and sufficient liquid TLOS. RAM uses `eosio::buyram` with a fixed quantity: the debit is bounded, while the number of bytes received depends on the market. CPU/NET use `eosio::delegatebw` with `transfer=false`, leaving ownership of the new stake with the payer.

These are direct system transactions. RAM becomes the recipient's account RAM and creates no DAO purchase credit; there is no Daclify operational fee on this path. CPU/NET remain delegations owned by the payer. Funding an account grants no membership, governance, upgrade or account authority. The card provides no RAM sale or unstaking flow.

Prepared purchases expire after 60 seconds. Changing account, resource, amount, network or wallet invalidates the preview. Closing the card or leaving the tab cancels outstanding signing intent before broadcast; an already submitted transaction cannot be recalled. The app rechecks the system code/ABI hashes, chain, balance and signing context before sending, and shows **Confirmed** only after a matching execution receipt. If submission is uncertain, check the displayed transaction ID in the wallet before acknowledging it and preparing another purchase. The app never retries a purchase automatically. **Refresh** updates the account readings and market. Escape closes the modal and restores focus to **Resources**.

The permission explorer continues to read public metadata and does not change authorities. Resource purchases require the user's wallet approval.
